What changes once you apply

How debt review works

Five steps from your first call to your clearance certificate. We do the paperwork. You keep paying one amount.

  1. Free assessment

    You talk us through your income, essential expenses and debts. In person in Wynberg, by phone, or with Q, our online assistant.

  2. Protection starts

    We apply for debt review and notify your creditors and the credit bureaus. From here, accounts in the process can't be handed over for legal action.

  3. Negotiation

    We draw up a restructured plan with lower instalments and interest, and negotiate it with every creditor.

  4. Court order

    The plan becomes a court order, so it's legally binding. You pay one amount each month through a registered payment distribution agency.

  5. Clearance certificate

    When the debts are settled, you get a clearance certificate and the debt review flag comes off your credit record.

Questions people ask us

Will I lose my car or my house?

Once you apply for debt review and your creditors are notified, they can't start legal action to enforce debts included in the process, as long as you keep to your plan. Home loans and vehicle finance can be included. If you've already received a summons or a garnishee order, call us straight away.

What does it cost?

Your first assessment is free. Debt counselling fees are set by the National Credit Regulator's guidelines and built into your monthly plan payment, so you don't pay a large amount upfront. We explain every cost in writing before you sign.

Can I take new credit while I'm under debt review?

No. You can't take on new credit while under debt review. This protects you from falling further behind while your plan runs.

How long does debt review take?

It depends on what you owe and what you can afford. Many plans run for about three to five years. Your counsellor will show you a realistic timeline at your assessment.

How do I get out of debt review?

When all the debts in your plan are paid, except a home loan, your counsellor issues a clearance certificate and the flag is removed from your credit record. In some cases, removal is possible earlier.

Am I blacklisted?

South Africa has no official blacklist. Credit bureaus keep your payment history, and lenders use it to decide. A poor record can be repaired with steady payments over time.
